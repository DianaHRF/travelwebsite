Arrival

We gave the Fuji Marriott Hotel Lake Yamanaka two nights, drawn by its spot up in the Fuji Five Lakes region. Getting there is a small adventure in itself: a bus from Mishima Station’s South Gate to Fujisan Yamanakako, where the hotel shuttle scooped us up for the last ten-minute leg. The hotel is a 5* that opened back in July 2017. We had booked a premium king room with its own private hot spring for around £165 a night and, with the place fully booked, no upgrade came our way this time — no matter, that private onsen was the reason we came.

Room

Bedroom

The room came in at 42 square metres, nicely designed, with a large TV facing the bed and a wall of forest filling the window. One quirk worth flagging: there is no balcony — not in ours, and not in any of the rooms.

Bathroom

The bathroom itself was on the modest side, but that hardly registered — because tucked into it was our own private onsen, comfortably one of the highlights of the whole stay.

Amenities

The usual comforts were all present and a notch above: bathrobes, flip flops and umbrellas, plus properly good tea and coffee to see us through the quiet evenings.

Food

Breakfast

Breakfast was a buffet with a foot in both camps, Japanese and Western. There were several kinds of fish, cold cuts and a soup station, and the fruit and pastries were particularly good. All told, a high-quality spread with plenty of choice to fuel a day on the trails.

Facilities

Lounge

The lounge here was unlike any we had used before. A little special tea and coffee appeared through the day, but its real purpose came at dinner — a takeaway buffet (yes, takeaway only). They handed out trays to carry the food back to the room, along with a choice of drinks to take with it. The food itself was nothing to write home about, but with no restaurants nearby it was a welcome way to keep hunger at bay and end the evening content.

Gym and onsen

There was a well-equipped gym and even ping pong tables, but the real draw was the outdoor mineral hot spring — a proper open-air onsen, with separate areas for men and women.

The verdict

This is a hotel in the shadow of Mt Fuji, so the location is a gift for anyone who loves nature and hiking. Reaching it without a car takes a bit of effort — a train and then a bus — but once you are there the rooms are spacious, the breakfast is very good, and the whole place has a wonderfully relaxing feel. Our one tip: book a room with a private hot spring. You won’t regret it.
