Arrival

Our Japan adventure kicked off in Nagasaki, and we picked the Nagasaki Marriott for three nights. It is a one-minute walk from the train station — and we do mean one minute, not the optimistic kind — which was a genuine gift given it was absolutely bucketing down when we arrived. The hotel is a 5* that opened in January 2024, and it never felt crowded (Nagasaki is far less touristy than Japan’s bigger names), so first impressions were lovely. We had booked a regular king room, but our Titanium status with Marriott Bonvoy quietly bumped us up to the Kamome suite for the whole stay — and what a suite: 86 square metres of minimalist, beautifully judged design.

The curtains were all electric, and the next morning they slid back to reveal the train station, the city and the surrounding hills laid out below — the sort of view that makes you forget the coffee for a minute.

Room

Bedroom

The bedroom was cosy, with a large TV facing the bed and, again, those views. The bed itself was excellent — the sort you sink into and immediately renegotiate your morning plans.

Amenities

There were bathrobes, flip flops and umbrellas on hand, and the tea and coffee were a cut above the usual. A little plate of traditional Japanese cake was waiting for us as a welcome gift — the kind of small, thoughtful touch we always notice.

Balcony

The suite wrapped around the corner with a big balcony — no chairs or table to actually sit at, sadly, but the views out over the city more than made up for it.

Bathroom

The bathroom was gloriously spacious and modern, with his-and-hers sinks, and the suite had two toilets — one in the main bathroom and one nearer the entrance (both Japanese, naturally, with all the buttons one could wish for).

Food

Now, breakfast. The restaurant was spacious with a beautiful view, and the buffet was genuinely high quality, much of it locally sourced. The fish, miso soup, tofu and pickles were outstanding, and there was an automatic dispenser for iced matcha latte, which frankly felt like a personal gift to us. All told, it was the best hotel breakfast we had anywhere in Japan — worth setting an alarm for.

Facilities

The lounge was one of the nicest Marriott lounges we have parked ourselves in. Good coffee and tea flowed all day, with nibbles appearing at various points, and it all came with a lovely city view and pleasant music — a properly relaxed spot to while away an hour. The food was high quality, if not exactly generous in choice.

The verdict

This ended up being our favourite hotel in Japan so far, and it is not hard to see why. The location is almost impossible to beat — it is basically in the train station. The area is quieter and less touristy than most, the service was every inch the 5* it claims, and the suite, the views, the breakfast and the lounge were all a joy. In short: if you are spending a few nights in Nagasaki, this is the one.
